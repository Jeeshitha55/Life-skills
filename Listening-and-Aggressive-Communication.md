# Listening and Active Communication

## 1. Active Listening

### Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?

1. Pay attention to the person who is speaking.
2. Look at the person while they are talking.
3. Do not interrupt them in the middle of a conversation.
4. Listen carefully and try to understand what they are saying.
5. Ask questions if you don't understand anything.
6. Give a proper response to show that you are listening.
7. Remember the important points they share.
   
## 2. Reflective Listening

### Question 2: According to Fisher’s model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?

Reflective listening means listening carefully to someone and then saying their message in my own words. It helps the speaker feel heard and helps me make sure I understood them correctly.

- **Pay full attention:** I should listen carefully without getting distracted or interrupting.
- **Understand the message:** I should try to understand both the facts and the feelings behind what the person is saying.
- **Repeat the message:** I can say, “So, you feel worried because the project deadline is close. Is that right?”
- **Check my understanding:** I should ask the speaker if my understanding is correct.
- **Show respect and empathy:** I should accept the speaker’s feelings without judging or criticizing them.
- **Let them correct me:** If I understood wrongly, the speaker can explain again and I can listen carefully.
- **Complete the communication:** Reflective listening reduces misunderstanding because both people know the message has been understood.

## 3. Reflection

### Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

- I sometimes get distracted by my phone, notifications, or my own thoughts.
- I may start thinking about my reply while the other person is still speaking.
- When I already think I know the answer, I may stop listening carefully.
- I can lose focus during long explanations or technical discussions.
- Sometimes I interrupt because I want to share my opinion quickly.
- When I am tired, stressed, or upset, I may not listen patiently.
- I may assume what the other person means instead of asking them to explain.
- Background noise or doing many things at the same time can make it hard to concentrate.

### Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

- Keep my phone on silent and avoid doing other work during important conversations.
- Make eye contact and use positive body language to show interest.
- Let the speaker finish before I respond.
- Take notes during meetings and technical discussions.
- Ask questions when I do not understand something.
- Repeat or summarize important points to check my understanding.
- Stay calm and patient, especially during difficult discussions.
- Avoid judging the speaker before they finish speaking.
- Practice active listening every day with friends, family, and teammates.

## 4. Types of Communication

### Question 5: When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I may use passive communication when I want to avoid conflict or when I do not feel confident enough to share my opinion. For example, if a teammate gives me extra work, I may say “yes” even when I am already busy because I do not want to disappoint them.

I may also become passive when talking to seniors, teachers, or clients because I feel nervous or think my opinion may not matter. In these situations, I avoid saying “no,” do not share my real thoughts, and let others make decisions for me.

### Question 6: When do you switch into Aggressive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I may become aggressive when I feel angry, frustrated, or under pressure. For example, if someone keeps interrupting me or ignores my request, I may raise my voice, use harsh words, or blame them directly.

This can also happen when I am stressed about a deadline or feel that someone is being unfair. In aggressive communication, I focus only on my own point of view and may not respect the other person’s feelings or rights.

### Question 7: When do you switch into Passive-Aggressive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I may become passive-aggressive when I am upset but do not want to say directly what is bothering me. Instead of clearly saying, “I am unhappy about this,” I may use sarcasm, make indirect comments, complain to someone else, or give silent treatment.

### Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive?

I can make my communication more assertive by expressing my thoughts, needs, and feelings clearly while still respecting the other person.
- **Use “I” statements:** Instead of saying, “You never listen to me,” I can say, “I feel unheard when I am interrupted. I would like to finish my point.”
- **Be clear and specific:** I should clearly say what I need. For example, “Please send the updated file by tomorrow morning.”
- **Stay calm:** I can speak in a steady and respectful voice, and avoid shouting or using hurtful words.
- **Use confident body language:** I can make eye contact, sit or stand straight, and avoid crossing my arms.
- **Listen to the other person:** Being assertive also means listening to their side and trying to find a solution together.
- **Practice regularly:** I can start with small situations, like politely disagreeing or asking for help, and slowly use these skills in more difficult conversations.
