# Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH)

## What kinds of behaviour cause sexual harassment?

Sexual harassment means unwanted behaviour related to sex that makes a person feel uncomfortable, unsafe, or disrespected at work. It can happen through words, actions, messages, or physical contact.

Some examples are:

- Making sexual jokes or comments.
- Passing comments about someone’s body, clothes, or looks.
- Sending sexual messages, pictures, or videos.
- Staring, whistling, or making sexual gestures.
- Touching, hugging, or standing too close without permission.
- Asking someone for dates or sexual favours again and again.
- Threatening someone’s job, promotion, or appraisal if they refuse sexual demands.
- Spreading rumours about someone’s personal life.
- Showing or sharing sexual pictures or videos at work.

Even if someone says it was “just a joke,” it can still be sexual harassment if the other person feels uncomfortable or did not agree to it.

## What would you do if you face or see such behaviour?

If I face or see sexual harassment, I would not ignore it. I would take action in a safe and responsible way.

- **If it happens to me:** I would clearly tell the person to stop. If I feel unsafe, I would move away and inform someone I trust.
- **If I see it happening:** I would support the person, make sure they are safe, and report the incident.
- **Save proof:** I would keep messages, screenshots, emails, dates, times, and names of people who saw it.
- **Report it:** I would inform the POSH Committee, HR, or my manager as soon as possible.
- **Do not stay silent:** Ignoring harassment can allow it to happen again to me or someone else.
- **Do not retaliate:** I would not threaten, blame, or trouble the person who reported the incident.
- **Keep it private:** I would share the details only with the right people who handle the complaint.

If the behaviour happens again, I would report every incident and ask for support. Everyone deserves to work in a safe, respectful, and professional place.
